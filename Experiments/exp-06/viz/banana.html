Step 1: the world on the banana

The banana is a slot bent along a circular arc, sized like Honshu at its Kantō width: arc A = 80°, centreline radius R = 237 ri (about 1,300 km along the axis), width w = 60 ri (about 236 km, coast to coast across Kantō). It is turned so the top tip of the centreline heads due north (90°), which puts the outer apex facing ESE, the Pacific side. The outline is walked anticlockwise from the outer apex, and θ is the fraction of its length walked, in degrees. The world, 36 ri flat to flat and north-up, is centred on the outline point at θ moved inland by d. Units are ri.

the whole banana · θ marked every 30° · north up
round the world · edges read as sea, land or crossed

The edge reading: an edge the outline crosses is a shore edge; an edge it does not cross is sea or land by where its midpoint lies. The rule from the napkin, no more than four of either, is checked live. The two shore crossings are the ends of the captured edge, the seed of step 3's shoreline.
