Step 1: the world on the banana

The banana is a slot bent along a circular arc, sized like Honshu at its Kantō width: arc A = 80°, centreline radius R = 237 ri (about 1,300 km along the axis), width w = 60 ri (about 236 km, coast to coast across Kantō). It is turned so the top tip of the centreline heads due north (90°), which puts the outer apex facing ESE, the Pacific side. The outline is walked anticlockwise from the outer apex, and θ is the fraction of its length walked, in degrees. The world, 36 ri flat to flat and north-up, is centred on the outline point at θ moved inland by d. Units are ri.

the whole banana · θ marked every 30° · north up
round the world · edges read as sea, land or crossed

The edge reading: an edge the outline crosses is a shore edge; an edge it does not cross is sea or land by where its midpoint lies. The rule from the napkin, no more than four of either, is checked live. The two shore crossings are the ends of the captured edge, the seed of the coarse shoreline in step 4.

Step 2: tectonics, as two curves

Height anywhere on the band is h = H(s) · f(a). f(a) is one cross-section, the same everywhere: a runs from +1 at the outer coast to −1 at the inner, a wide plain on the outer side, the spine a little past the middle, a steep drop to the inner coast, 0 at both coasts. H(s) is the spine's height along the centreline, low at the tips and highest round the bend. Round the caps the cross-section is stretched to the semicircle, so the shore stays at 0. Sea is not computed. The sliders are the same θ and d as above, wherever they appear.

The cross-section is a smooth spline through a short table of control points, so it is a thing to edit rather than a formula to tune; the spine curve is a raised sine along the centreline. Both are smooth, so the erosion filter of a later step can take a gradient off them anywhere. In layers (5 sun each) the numbers are about 6.6 per metre.

Step 3: climate, ri by ri

Four numbers per ri from what the band already knows: where it is (northing for latitude, a for which coast, distance from the coast for continentality), how high it is, and which way it slopes. Temperature: a sea-level mean by latitude, 15.5 °C at the apex falling 0.036 °C per ri northward, minus 6.5 °C per 1,000 m; the coldest and warmest months sit a half-range either side of it, 10.5 °C at the coast widening to 13 °C well inland. Precipitation: 1,500 mm on the Pacific side blending to 2,200 on the Japan Sea side, plus 0.5 mm per metre of height, plus up to ±600 mm for slopes facing into or away from the winter NW monsoon and the summer SE rains.

Every term is a coefficient in the script, stylised from Honshu's stations: Tokyo 15.4 °C and 1,600 mm, Niigata 13.6 °C and 1,800 mm, Matsumoto 11.8 °C and 1,030 mm, Aomori 10.4 °C. The slope term needs a gradient, taken here by finite differences half a ri apart; the erosion filter later will want the same gradient, analytically. Sea ri are left blank.
