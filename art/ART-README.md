# Art: Murabito's 3D models

Every model in `assets/models/` is **generated by a Python script run inside Blender**,
headless. Nobody opens the Blender UI. Each script builds its mesh from numbers
(proportions, rings, seeds), paints each face from a shared colour palette, and exports
a `.glb` that Bevy loads directly. Changing a model means changing numbers and re-running
its script, so every change is a readable diff.

- Scripts live in `art/`, and tools for checking the models in `art/tools/`. They are
  code, under MIT.
- What they produce lives in `assets/models/`. That is an asset, under CC BY-SA 4.0, stored
  through Git LFS (`.glb` and `.png` are already routed there by `.gitattributes`).

## Quick start

You need Blender (5.0 is what's been used). From the repo root:

```sh
# an animal
blender -b --python art/fox.py -- --out assets/models/fox.glb

# a plant: pick a version, colour variant and season (each has a default)
blender -b --python art/maple.py -- --version 00 --colour a --season summer \
    --out assets/models/maple-00-a-summer.glb

# add a preview sheet (front, side, top and three-quarter views in one PNG)
blender -b --python art/sakura.py -- --season spring --out /tmp/x.glb --renders <dir>
```

Everything after `--` goes to the script. `--renders <dir>` writes `<dir>/<name>.png`, a
2×2 sheet (front | side over top | three-quarter), plus each view as its own file beside
it. The script prints the model's face and triangle counts.

Harmless noise: the glTF exporter logs `ERROR Draco mesh compression is not available`.
It is only reporting that an optional compression library is missing; we don't use it.

## The house style

These are the rules agreed while making the first models. New models should follow them
unless there's a reason not to.

- **Cutesy, faceted low-poly** for a Japanese rural village around 1550. Every face is flat
  shaded (no smoothing), so the facets show.
- **Halfway between natural and chibi.** Animals get a head about 1.3× natural, big ears
  and tails, and a hint of chibi, never enough to look unnatural. Plants get the same
  treatment: recognisable species, simplified and slightly exaggerated.
- **Full, even bodies.** No thin or hourglass (waisted) bodies; legs stand fairly straight
  under the body (a zig-zag, hunched hind leg was rejected).
- **Colour comes from the shared palette** (see below), with **per-triangle variation**
  on foliage and fur-like surfaces, never flat single-colour areas where life would vary.
- **Even triangle size across a surface.** Mixing small and large triangles on one canopy
  looks wrong, so foliage skins are sized to a target triangle edge (see *Triangle size*).
- **Trees read from above.** The game camera is mostly overhead, so silhouettes from the
  top view matter as much as from the side.
- **Right for the period.** Pick what a 16th-century village would actually have had,
  and say so in the script's docstring. For example: mountain cherry (yamazakura), not
  Somei-yoshino, which is from the 1800s; madake bamboo, not mōsō, which arrived in the
  1700s; the Japanese hare, not the European rabbit.
- **Don't name games** that inspired the look, in code or docs.

## Conventions

- **Units are shaku** (尺, about 30.3 cm). One Blender unit is one shaku. Metres appear
  only in comments for a sense of scale.
- **Axes:** models are built facing Blender **+Y** with **+Z up**, and the ground at
  z = 0. The glTF exporter turns that into Bevy's forward (−Z) and up (+Y).
- **Names:**
  - Animals: `<animal>.glb` (`fox.glb`).
  - Plants: `<plant>-<version>-<colour>-<season>.glb` (`maple-00-a-summer.glb`).
    - **version** is the shape: `00`, `01`, … (a special shape can have a name, like
      `pruned-00`).
    - **colour** is the colour variant, meaning the kind of plant: `a` is the default
      (green), then `b`, `c`… (the maples run green, orange, purple, red, black-red).
    - **season** picks that variant's colours (or bareness) for the time of year:
      `spring`, `summer`, `winter`, and `autumn` when needed.
- **Rig-ready animals:** each animal is one connected mesh with a ring of vertices at every
  joint (shoulders, elbows, knees, neck, tail, ears), so a skeleton can bend it later.
  None are rigged yet.
- **Buried trunks:** every tree's trunk reaches 1.5 shaku below ground (`flora.ROOT_DEPTH`)
  with its bottom rings level, so it sits on sloping terrain with no gap. The bamboo is
  scaled down whole (`SCALE`), so its culms reach only 0.6 shaku below.
- **Real sizes:** models are sized to real Japanese species (shoulder heights for animals,
  sourced figures for plants), trees as younger, well-used village trees rather than old
  giants. A script that is built in its own numbers and then sized states it once, as
  `SCALE` with its source, passed to `finish(scale=...)`; a few (fox, crane, dog) are
  sized by eye beside the others, and say so.

## The palette

All colour comes from `PALETTE` in `art/style.py`: a list of `(name, "#RRGGBB")` swatches.
Each swatch is one pixel of a 16×16 texture (`PALETTE_SIZE`), and every face points all
its UVs at the centre of its swatch's pixel, sampled with no blending (a `NEAREST`
sampler), so faces get exactly the swatch colour.

- **Append only.** A swatch's position in the list is its identity (its pixel), so add new
  swatches at the end, or after their family. Never reorder or delete: that would recolour
  existing models. Renaming is safe.
- **Retuning a colour is a one-line change.** Edit its hex value and re-export the models
  that use it.
- **Every `.glb` embeds the whole palette texture.** Any palette change (even adding a
  swatch another model never uses) changes the bytes of every model. To keep committed
  models carrying the current palette, **re-export all of them when the palette changes**
  (their geometry and colours don't change, only the embedded texture): run
  `art/tools/check.sh <dir>`, and if it reports `ALL MATCH`, copy `<dir>/rebuild/*.glb`
  into `assets/models/`.
- The texture holds 256 swatches; about 100 are used. If it fills, raise `PALETTE_SIZE`
  (`palette_image` asserts before it overflows). That changes every model's UVs, so
  re-export everything, as happened when it grew from 8×8.
- Name swatches by what they're for: `sakura_petal`, `pine_bark_red`, `leaf_green_shade`.

## The shared modules

### `style.py`: what every model shares

| | |
|---|---|
| `PALETTE`, `SWATCH`, `PALETTE_SIZE` | the palette, name → index, texture size |
| `SHADES` | fur and feather colours → the swatch plus its `_light` and `_deep` shades, for shaded animals |
| `reset_scene()` | start from an empty Blender scene |
| `apply_swatches(obj, names)` | give a mesh the palette material and point each face's UVs at its swatch |
| `export_glb(obj, path)` | export one object as `.glb` (Y-up) |
| `render_sheet(target, extent, out_png)` | the four-view preview sheet (Workbench engine, colours shown as written) |

### `loft.py`: building by lofting, and the command line

| | |
|---|---|
| `Builder` | a bmesh whose faces each remember a swatch: `face(verts, colour)`, `paint(faces, colour)`, `stud(centre, normal, size, colour, sides=4, tall=1.0)` (a small closed stud standing on a surface: eyes, spots; more sides make it rounder, `tall` stretches it up and down), `finish(name, shaded=False, scale=1.0)` → a coloured object; `shaded=True` varies each fur or feather face among its `SHADES`, from a stream seeded by `name`; `scale` sizes the whole model about the origin (feet stay on the ground) |
| `Builder.spine(start_tip, rings, end_tip, upright=0)` | loft an animal's body along a spine in the YZ plane, octagonal rings from tail tip to nose (an optional seventh value tapers a ring, wider at the top: a trapezoid face), each square to the spine except the `upright` ones (a count from the start, or ring indices), which stand straight up (a tail fan, a rump under a high tail); returns the faces per segment |
| `Builder.limb(face, rings, mirror, tip)` | replace one of those faces with a limb (leg, ear) lofted through square rings; returns its faces |
| `run(build, name, target, extent)` | the shared command line (`--out`, `--renders`); scripts parse their own extra flags first |

The octagon faces are numbered 0 upper right, 1 top, 2 upper left, 3 left, 4 lower left,
5 bottom, 6 lower right, 7 right (right being +X). Limbs grow from those faces.

### `flora.py`: plants

Structure:

| | |
|---|---|
| `branch(b, points, radii, colour, upright=0)` | a closed six-sided tube through a 3D polyline; `colour` can be one swatch or one per segment; the first `upright` rings stay level (for trunk bases); returns the faces per segment |
| `densify(points, radii, step)` | extra points so none are more than `step` apart |
| `gnarl(points, radii, rng, amount, step)` | knotty, kinked version of a polyline (ends stay put) |
| `sink(points, radii)` | add a buried point under a trunk's base (draw with `upright=2`) |
| `point_at_height(points, z)` | where an upright polyline passes a height |
| `grow_branches(trunk_points, rules, rng)` | branches spiralling up a trunk by the golden angle, from rules (count, heights, lengths, pads, forks, and optionally rise, droop, wander, thickness, taper, lift); returns each branch and where its foliage goes |
| `conifer(b, spec, needles, shades, bark)` | a whole conifer from a spec: trunk, grown branches, tufts or clustered crown, tip |

Foliage:

| | |
|---|---|
| `canopy(b, centre, lumps, leaves, shades, rng, …)` | **shrink-wrap**: one skin over a set of lumps `(centre, radius, up, down)`, made by casting rays out from `centre`; `shape=` squashes the base sphere for flat skins; `triangles=` sets the triangle count; returns the faces |
| `even_triangles(centre, lumps, shape, edge)` | how many triangles a skin needs for a given edge length, from a trial wrap that measures its real surface |
| `pad(...)` | a flat lumpy pad of needles (the red pine's) |
| `wrap_sprays(b, group, …)` | wrap a group of sprays as one skin with even triangles |
| `clustered_crown(b, sprays, …, tier, sectors)` | cut a crown into tiers and sectors, one skin each (the hinoki's) |
| `clump(...)` | an old-style separate blob (only the cloud-pruned maple uses it) |
| `blade(...)`, `blade_cluster(...)` | flat, pointed leaves, ridged down the middle, fanning in drooping clusters (the bamboo's) |

Colouring: skins pick a colour per face at random from `leaves`, or from `shades` if the
face points down. Repeat a colour in a list to make it more likely.

## Making a new model

The loop that has worked: **describe the plan, build it, render the sheet, look at it
critically yourself, fix what's clearly off, then show Lexa and iterate on their
feedback.** One or two changes per round. Most first passes miss in a predictable way
(too thin, too sparse, the wrong silhouette), so check the render against the house style
before showing it.

### An animal

1. Copy the closest animal (`fox.py` for four-legged and standing, `hare.py` for crouched
   with long ears, `crane.py` for a bird on two legs with a long neck).
2. Edit `SPINE`: rings from tail tip to nose as `(y, z, half-width, half-height, colour,
   underside colour)`. Keep neighbouring rings in proportion: a ring bigger than both its
   neighbours shows as a ridge.
3. Set `HIND_SEGMENT`, `FRONT_SEGMENT`, `EAR_SEGMENT` to the spine segments limbs grow
   from, and edit `FRONT_LEG`, `HIND_LEG`, `EAR` (rings of `(x, y, z, half-width,
   half-depth, colour)` for the right side; the left is mirrored).
4. Patches that don't follow rings (calico, masks): paint them after building with
   `b.paint(...)` on spine or limb faces (see `cat.py`). Limb faces come back from
   `b.limb(...)`. Markings smaller than a face (spots) are small closed studs set on
   a face (`deer.py`'s `spot`): painting whole faces for them reads as a checkerboard.
   Rigid parts that branch (antlers) can be `flora.branch` tubes rooted in the body.
5. Finish with `b.finish(name, shaded=True)`, so the coat varies a little face by face.
   A new coat colour needs `_light` and `_deep` swatches beside it and an entry in
   `SHADES`; small details (a crown, a bill, legs) can stay a single swatch.
6. Add any new swatches to the palette, build, render, iterate.

### A plant

Start from whichever existing plant is closest:

| For | Start from | Its crown |
|---|---|---|
| a broadleaf with one lumpy crown | `maple.py` | one `canopy` skin over hand-placed lumps (a mushroom-like cap with rim and top lumps) |
| a broadleaf on spreading limbs | `sakura.py` | a skin of masses on each limb, plus fill on top; gnarled trunk; bare winter with twigs |
| a pine: crooked, sparse, flat pads | `redpine.py` | `grow_branches` + a `pad` on each branch end |
| a conifer: straight spire of tufts | `sugi.py` | `conifer` with round tufts |
| a conifer: broad, layered fans | `hinoki.py` | `conifer` with `clustered_crown` |
| grasses, reeds, bamboo, anything with narrow leaves | `bamboo.py` | `blade_cluster`s on culms |
| a low shrub | `azalea.py` | one `canopy` mound on the ground, with its triangle size scaled down |

Then:

1. Write the docstring first: species (with its Japanese name), size in shaku, what makes
   its silhouette distinctive, and any period notes.
2. Add swatches: a few leaf colours plus underside shades, and bark.
3. Fill in `COLOURS` (variant → season → colour lists) and `VERSIONS["00"]`.
4. Build and render. Compare with the other trees: a new plant should read as *different*
   from its neighbours (the hinoki's first pass looked like the sugi, and had to change
   shape, foliage and colour to stand apart).

## Versions, colour variants and seasons

- **Versions** are entries in the script's `VERSIONS`. Hand-placed ones (`maple` `00`,
  `sakura` `00`) are literal data. Generated ones use a small function (`_wild`,
  `_grown`) that grows the trunk and limbs from a seed and a few knobs (height, lean,
  branch count, reach, gnarl). Five versions per plant (`00`–`04`) has been the norm; show
  them side by side before committing.
- **Colour variants** only change `COLOURS`. The shape stays the same.
- **Seasons** are colour lists inside a variant. Flowers can be colour alone (the
  azalea's blossom patches, the sakura's pink crown). A **bare** season is `None`, and the
  script builds twigs instead of foliage (the sakura's winter).

### The random stream: keep seasons and variants the same shape

Each build draws everything (lump shapes, jitter, colour picks) from one seeded random
stream, in order. Anything that changes how many draws happen early on changes the shape
of everything after it. Rules that follow from that:

- **Within a variant, every leafy season's colour lists must be the same lengths.** A
  random pick from a longer list can use more draws. `sakura.py` asserts this.
- **Anything added underneath the existing shape must not draw from the stream.** The
  buried trunk segments take a fixed colour for that reason.
- **Extra geometry for one season uses its own stream** (the sakura's twigs), and a
  season that drops geometry still draws it into a throwaway builder, so the rest stays
  in step (the sakura's bare winter).
- **Generated versions take their trunk from a separate stream** (`seed * 7919 + 1`),
  apart from the build's.

When changing shared code, **prove existing models didn't change** with
`art/tools/check.sh` (see *Checking your work*). Every refactor so far was checked that
way.

## Triangle size

- **Trees:** foliage skins aim for a triangle edge of `flora.TRI_EDGE` (1.8 shaku; the
  maple's is about 1.9). `even_triangles` measures a trial wrap and picks the count, so
  skins of any size come out even. Older skins (pine pads, sugi tufts) use icospheres,
  whose counts only come in steps of four, so they run a little finer (about 1.3–1.4).
- **Shrubs** scale the edge down (`FACET = 0.6` in `azalea.py`), or a small plant gets only
  a few dozen chunky facets.
- **Current costs:** animals 370–450 triangles (the deer, with spots and antlers, about 1,000); shrubs about 900; broadleaf trees
  1,500–6,600; conifers 4,500–10,000; bamboo up to 17,600. That's accepted for now: the
  plan is to come back and make **low-LoD versions** of everything to fade to at distance.

## Checking your work

The tools in `art/tools/` do the checking. Run them from anywhere in the repo; the
Blender ones take their arguments after `--`, and the shell ones find the repo root
themselves.

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| `check.sh <dir> [--above Z]` | rebuilds every committed model into `<dir>/rebuild` and compares each, face by face, with the committed file: `ALL MATCH`, or the models that differ. `--above Z` compares only faces wholly above height Z, for a change meant to touch only what's below (burying trunks was checked with it). Takes under a minute. |
| `rebuild.sh <dir>` | rebuilds every committed model into `<dir>`, working out each one's script and flags from its file name. `check.sh` uses it; on its own it's how to re-export everything after a palette change. |
| `fingerprint.py -- [--above Z] <glb>…` | the face-by-face fingerprint `check.sh` compares: each face's corners and the palette colour its UVs land on, so two exports match even when their UVs or embedded palette differ. |
| `ground.py -- <glb>…` | each model's lowest heights: trunks should bottom out at −1.5, animals' soles at 0, a shrub's skirt a little below 0. |
| `sheet.py -- --renders <dir> --out <png> <name>…` | the front and three-quarter views of several rendered models side by side, to compare versions, colours or seasons. |
| `zoom.py -- <in.png> <out.png> <x> <y> <w> <h> [scale]` | crop part of a render and enlarge it. |

For example:

```sh
art/tools/check.sh /tmp/art-check                    # did my change leave every model alone?
cp /tmp/art-check/rebuild/*.glb assets/models/       # after a palette change: take the re-exports
blender -b --python art/tools/ground.py -- assets/models/sugi-00-a-summer.glb
```

And by eye:

- **Look at every render sheet**, and zoom in on anything subtle (bark blends, ear
  bases, branch tips). Several problems only showed up close.
- **The top view matters:** it's close to what the game camera sees.
- **Seasons share a shape:** a variant's seasons should report the same triangle count.
  If they don't, the random stream diverged (see above).

## Lessons from what didn't work

- **Lumpy skins can't make narrow leaves.** Bamboo went through columns, pom-poms and
  floating blobs before switching to leaf blades.
- **Separate blobs on long bare branches read as an acacia or a stone pine.** It happened
  to the maple and the sakura both. Broadleaf crowns need to come down and close up.
- **Blobs of different sizes cut into each other.** Wrap them as one skin
  (`canopy`/`wrap_sprays`) instead of overlapping separate spheres.
- **Shrink-wrapping needs every ray to hit something.** Give a group a core lump at its
  middle (`wrap_sprays` does), and keep the lumps roughly star-shaped around the centre.
- **Flat pads need their branch tips inside them**, or the twigs poke out (`lift` in
  `grow_branches`).
- **Random colour per triangle looks like camouflage for flowers.** Blossom goes in
  patches.
- **Ball-on-sticks shrubs look like topiary.** Wild shrubs sit on the ground.
- **Preview frames are fixed:** a tall version can clip at the top. Frame by the model's
  own height when versions differ (`sugi.py`, `hinoki.py` do).
- **A limb's first ring must fit inside the face it grows from.** Where a thick thigh
  reaches past its base face, front or back, it tucks under the body and leaves a notch
  (the deer took several rounds). Make the segment it grows from as long as the thigh is
  deep, by dropping a ring if needed, and keep the ring inside the body's width too, or
  its square corner pokes out.
- **Rings square to a sharply curving spine swing past each other and fold**, leaving a
  hook on the inside of the curve (the rooster's tail), or a point under a high tail (the
  dog's rump). Stand those rings upright (`spine(..., upright=...)`).
- **A ring whose underside hangs below both neighbours' shows as a point** (the macaque's
  chin), just as one that bulges out on top shows as a ridge. Measure each ring's bottom
  edge to find it; guessing at the rings around it didn't work.
- **A stud on a four-sided face can come out bigger on one side than its mirror.** The
  face isn't flat, and mirrored faces can split into triangles along different diagonals,
  sinking one stud deeper (the wolf's eyes). Poke the face first and set the stud on
  its centre.
- **An eye bigger than its face, or standing tall, clips.** Space the rings round an eye
  so it fits its face, and keep eye studs low (`height`, `inset`) so they sit nearly
  flush (the pheasant's eyes).
- **A ring on a rising spine leans back, and its top sits lower than its numbers say.**
  To line up a topline, check the ring vertices, not the ring centres (the dog's withers).
- **A ring bigger than both neighbours shows as a ridge**, and a rump that ends in one
  ring looks chopped off. Round rumps over two or three rings, from the side and from
  above (the hare, the deer).
